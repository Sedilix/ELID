# ELID Domain Playbooks & Reference Library

This reference library contains specialized mental anchors, visual templates, and mechanical breakdowns across diverse knowledge domains.

---

## 1. Physical Sciences & Modern Physics

### Entanglement
* **Everyday Anchor:** Take a pair of red and blue socks. Put one into Box A and one into Box B without looking, and ship Box B to Australia. The exact split-second you open Box A in London and see a red sock, you instantly know Box B in Australia contains the blue sock—zero phone calls or light-speed radio signals needed.
* **Failure / Boundary Case:** Can you use this to send a message faster than light? No, because until you open your box, you cannot choose which color you get; you only see random noise until both ends compare notes.

### Special Relativity (Time Dilation)
* **Everyday Anchor:** Speed through space steals from your speed through time. Imagine driving a car on a massive flat plane at exactly 100 mph. If you head pure North (stationary in space), you move North at 100 mph (full speed through time). If you turn the wheel sharply North-East (moving fast through space), your Northward progress slows down. If you move at the speed of light through space, your time speedometer drops to zero.

## 2. Advanced Mathematics

### Matrix Multiplication
* **Everyday Anchor:** A recipe converter. You have 3 cookie recipes (rows) and each requires varying amounts of flour, sugar, and butter (columns). A matrix multiplication takes the cost of flour, sugar, and butter at 2 different grocery stores, and outputs the exact total cost of making each recipe at each store in a single operation.

### Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors
* **Everyday Anchor:** Stretching a rubber sheet with a drawing on it. Most lines on the drawing will twist and change direction as you pull the corners. But certain lines point in directions that stretch or shrink without turning at all. The directions that do not turn are the **Eigenvectors**, and the factor by which they grow or shrink is the **Eigenvalue**.

## 3. Computer Systems & Engineering

### DNS (Domain Name System)
* **Everyday Anchor:** The contacts list in your smartphone. You never memorize that your mother is reachable at phone number `+1-202-555-0143`; you tap "Mom" and your phone silently translates the human name into the telephone number network switches understand. DNS translates `example.com` into numeric IP addresses like `93.184.216.34`.

### Encryption & Public-Key Cryptography
* **Everyday Anchor:** A padlock with two keys. Anyone can pick up an open padlock, snap it shut on a metal box, and mail it to you. But once snapped shut, only the person who holds the private key can unlock it. The open padlocks are handed out freely to the entire world (public key); the key stays locked in your pocket (private key).

## 4. Medicine, Biology & Pharmacology

### Vaccines & mRNA
* **Everyday Anchor:** A wanted poster photocopied onto edible paper. Traditional vaccines introduce an inactive or weakened burglar into the police station so the guards can practice tackling him. mRNA vaccines simply slide a blueprint of the burglar's jacket under the precinct door. The guards build a dummy mannequin wearing the jacket, train their attack dogs on it, and immediately shred the blueprint paper.

### Antibiotic Resistance
* **Everyday Anchor:** Throwing weed-killer on a lawn where 99.9% of weeds die, but 3 mutant weeds with thicker skin survive. If you stop weeding halfway through, those 3 mutants reproduce without any competition and fill the entire garden with invulnerable super-weeds.

## 5. Law & Civics

### Hearsay
* **Everyday Anchor:** A game of telephone. In court, the judge wants to look the witness in the eye and evaluate if they are lying. If Alice testifies, "Bob told me that Charlie stole the car," the jury cannot cross-examine Bob (who said it), only Alice (who heard it). Therefore, the statement is excluded unless a strict reliability exception applies.

### Strict Liability
* **Everyday Anchor:** Keeping a pet tiger in your front yard. It does not matter how many warning signs you posted, how sturdy your fence was, or how carefully you locked the cage. If the tiger escapes and bites someone, you are automatically legally responsible, period—fault and good intentions are irrelevant.
